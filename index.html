OXAI 2026

Call for Contributions

How can we make the behaviour and decisions of AI systems observable, explainable, and accountable?

OXAI 2026 brings together researchers, practitioners, and other stakeholders to advance the state of the art and best practices in observability and explainability of AI systems. Observability provides the measurements, traces, and runtime evidence needed to understand internal states and behaviours. Explainability turns that evidence into understandable accounts of automated decisions, supporting trust, accountability, and compliance.

Building on OXAI 2024, this edition focuses on agentic AI systems that plan, use tools, maintain memory, and adapt under competing objectives. Particular attention will be given to explainable multi-objective optimization and to making trade-offs among quality, latency, cost, safety, privacy, security, sustainability, fairness, and accessibility observable and understandable.

We invite contributions on methods, architectures, tools, evaluations, governance mechanisms, and engineering practices. Our system-level perspective connects models and services with software architecture, cloud computing, data management, testing, operations, and responsible AI.

Topics of interest

Topics include, but are not limited to:

  • Observability in AI and Agent Systems: monitoring, logging, tracing, provenance, security telemetry, incident response, AgentOps/LLMOps/AIOps, and analysis of plans, memory, tool calls, interactions, and side effects.
  • Explainability in AI Engineering: methods for explaining models and system behaviour, human-centred and accessible explanations, causal diagnosis, and metrics for explanation quality.
  • Explainable Multi-Objective Optimization: agent planning, learning, and runtime adaptation under competing objectives; Pareto trade-offs; stakeholder-dependent preferences; and explanations of constraints and decisions.
  • Human-Centred Quality Attributes and Governance: integrating equity, diversity, and inclusion (EDI) by design, fairness, and accessibility into stakeholder requirements, data, interfaces, and evaluation, while addressing privacy, regulatory compliance, oversight, recourse, contestability, and societal impacts of opaque AI systems.
  • Case Studies, Tools, and Applications: empirical studies, benchmarks, demonstrations, experience reports, and industrial and cybersecurity applications of observable and explainable AI.

Participation and workshop format

We welcome academic scholars, industry professionals, government researchers, and community-facing practitioners, including data scientists, machine learning engineers, software architects and developers, AIOps/DevOps practitioners, and researchers in responsible AI, cybersecurity, and governance. Participants at all experience levels are welcome.

The planned full-day workshop combines invited presentations, community talks and demonstrations, a panel, and a facilitated breakout on competing stakeholder requirements and quality attributes. Participants will connect these requirements to observable system evidence and evaluation criteria. Planned outcomes include a concise engineering roadmap/checklist and a post-workshop activities-and-findings summary.

How to Submit

We accept two submission types:

  • Talk abstract: up to 2 pages, proposing a presentation at the workshop.
  • Paper: up to 5 pages, including all text, tables, figures, references, appendices, and any other content.

Submit a single PDF containing the contribution title, author names and affiliations, and your abstract or paper. Both page limits apply to the complete PDF.

Submissions are open through Microsoft CMT.Deadline: October 18, 2026, 23:59 Anywhere on Earth (UTC−12).

Submit through Microsoft CMT

Sign in to CMT or create a free account, then select Create new submission in the Author Console. Upload your PDF (maximum 10 MB), within the page limit for your submission type. For questions, contact the organizers.

Previously published, ongoing, and industrial work is welcome when clearly identified. Include a link to any prior publication when applicable.

No archival proceedings: Talk abstracts and papers are submitted for workshop presentation and discussion. Selected presentation slides may be shared publicly with the appropriate speaker and participant consent.
